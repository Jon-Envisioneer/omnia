# Omnia: an atlas of the adjacent possible

A speculative ontology of 65 paradigms across 13 realms and 4 time horizons. Each paradigm names what it would give reality, the signals already pointing toward it, and the shadow it would cast. Reach and strangeness are rough judgments, not forecasts. The listed signals are real as of 2026; everything else is speculation.

## Horizons

| Horizon | Span | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Within reach | 2026–2030 | Working prototypes exist. The work is adoption, craft and care. |
| Near horizon | 2030–2045 | The science is mostly known. Engineering, cost and consent stand in the way. |
| Far horizon | 2045–2100 | Needs one or two breakthroughs, or a shift in what people want. |
| Deep horizon | 2100 → ∞ | Physically allowed as far as we know. Possibly never reached. |

## Operators

Moves that generate new paradigms from existing ones.

- **Invert**: Reverse who acts and who is acted upon.
- **Rescale**: Move it up or down by six orders of magnitude.
- **Swap substrate**: Run the same idea on a different material: code, cells, stone, song.
- **Grant agency**: Give the passive part of the system wants of its own.
- **Dissolve a boundary**: Remove the line between two things everyone assumes are separate.
- **Make visible**: Expose what is currently hidden, implicit or unmeasured.
- **Change tempo**: Make it a thousand times slower or faster.
- **Commons-ify**: Turn the owned into the shared, or the shared into something stewarded.

## Realms

### Matter & Substrate

*Transforms what things are made of.* Stuff that computes, grows, heals and remembers where it came from.

#### Programmable Matter

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 62

Materials whose shape, stiffness, colour or conductivity are set by software and can be changed after manufacture.

- **Gives reality:** Objects update like apps. A chair becomes a step stool; a wing reshapes for each gust; repair is a recompile rather than a replacement.
- **Signals already here:** 4D printing of shape-memory polymers; mechanical metamaterials with tunable stiffness; modular self-reconfiguring robots
- **Shadow:** Planned obsolescence moves into the atoms; whoever holds the firmware owns the object.
- **Open question:** If every object could be re-flashed, which ones should be deliberately left un-programmable?

#### Metabolic Architecture

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 60 · strangeness 55

Buildings grown from living or once-living materials that breathe, self-repair and eventually compost.

- **Gives reality:** Construction switches from a large carbon source to a carbon sink, and cities gain a slow metabolism of their own.
- **Signals already here:** mycelium composite bricks and panels; bacterial self-healing concrete; engineered living materials research
- **Shadow:** Living walls can sicken. Maintenance turns into husbandry, which not every tenant can afford.
- **Open question:** What does a lease look like when the building is alive and has needs of its own?

#### Atomic Custody

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 40

Every material carries its provenance and next destination, so nothing leaves the loop as waste.

- **Gives reality:** Waste stops being a category. Mines become the backup for a circulating stock of materials that is already above ground.
- **Signals already here:** EU Digital Product Passport regulation; chemical recycling of mixed plastics; urban mining of e-waste
- **Shadow:** A full ledger of all matter is also a surveillance system for everything a person owns.
- **Open question:** Design the label on a spoon that has been melted and recast 400 times.

#### Designer Physics Pockets

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 30 · strangeness 85

Small engineered regions where light, sound or heat obey rules found nowhere in nature.

- **Gives reality:** A quiet room built from geometry rather than mass; heat that flows one way; optics that bend around a hospital bed.
- **Signals already here:** acoustic and optical cloaking metamaterials; time crystals observed in quantum processors; synthetic dimensions in photonics
- **Shadow:** Places where the usual rules fail are places where the usual safety assumptions fail too.
- **Open question:** What would you build if you could order a cubic metre of custom physics?

#### Matter Compilers

Deep horizon (2100 → ∞) · reach 12 · strangeness 92

Desktop machines that assemble almost any object, atom by atom, from a feedstock of common elements.

- **Gives reality:** Manufacturing becomes a file format. Scarcity of goods mostly ends, and the argument shifts to energy and design.
- **Signals already here:** DNA origami; automated chemical synthesis robots; de novo protein design
- **Shadow:** Anything printable includes weapons and pathogens; control shifts entirely to the feedstock and the files.
- **Open question:** In a world of matter compilers, what would people still insist on making by hand, and why?

### Mind & Cognition

*Transforms how thinking happens.* Thought spread across people, tools and models, and new ways to see it happening.

#### Cognitive Prosthetic Ecology

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 85 · strangeness 35

Thinking is distributed across a person, their notes, their tools and several models, each doing different cognitive work.

- **Gives reality:** Individuals can hold much larger problems in view. Expertise becomes less a stock of memorised facts and more a skill of orchestration.
- **Signals already here:** large language models as thinking partners; tools-for-thought software; agent harnesses with persistent memory
- **Shadow:** Unused abilities can fade, and whoever designs the tools shapes the thoughts people can easily reach.
- **Open question:** Which of your own mental skills would you refuse to outsource, even if a tool did it better?

#### Mind Federations

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 20 · strangeness 90

Two or more minds temporarily share working memory or perception, then separate again.

- **Gives reality:** A new kind of understanding between people: instead of describing an experience to someone, you let them share it.
- **Signals already here:** the Hogan twins, conjoined at the head, reporting shared sensation; high-bandwidth brain–computer interfaces; multi-agent AI with shared scratchpads
- **Shadow:** The boundaries of the self, consent and responsibility all blur at once.
- **Open question:** Write the consent form for a two-hour merge between strangers.

#### Synthetic Phenomenology

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 45 · strangeness 70

A rigorous science of what, if anything, it is like to be a non-human mind, whether animal, AI or engineered.

- **Gives reality:** A basis for moral decisions about minds unlike ours, grounded in something firmer than resemblance to humans.
- **Signals already here:** competing theories of consciousness tested head to head; mechanistic interpretability of neural networks; animal sentience reviews informing law
- **Shadow:** We may build a science that confidently answers the wrong question.
- **Open question:** What evidence would change your mind about whether a given system has experiences?

#### Attention Commons

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 45

Collective attention is treated as a shared resource, like a watershed, with rules against depleting it.

- **Gives reality:** Public life that is calmer and better at noticing slow problems, not just the loudest ones.
- **Signals already here:** digital wellbeing regulation; chronological and bridging feed options; school phone-free policies
- **Shadow:** Deciding what deserves attention is a short step from deciding what may be said.
- **Open question:** What would an environmental impact assessment for a new app measure?

#### Metacognitive Weather

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 55

A live, private forecast of your own cognitive state: fatigue, bias risk, mood fronts, focus windows.

- **Gives reality:** People schedule big decisions for clear skies and know when not to trust their own judgment.
- **Signals already here:** consumer EEG and heart-rate-variability wearables; language-based mood tracking; ecological momentary assessment in psychology
- **Shadow:** Employers and insurers will want the forecast too.
- **Open question:** What would you do differently tomorrow if you saw a 70% chance of confirmation bias after 3 pm?

### Life & Biosphere

*Transforms who counts as kin.* Treating the living world as a set of neighbours to negotiate with rather than resources to manage.

#### Interspecies Diplomacy

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 40 · strangeness 78

Machine learning helps decode animal communication well enough to exchange meaning, and eventually to negotiate.

- **Gives reality:** Humans stop being the only party at the table in decisions about shared habitat.
- **Signals already here:** Project CETI's analysis of sperm whale codas; Earth Species Project; bioacoustic monitoring networks
- **Shadow:** Being able to speak to animals also means being able to deceive and lure them.
- **Open question:** What is the first thing we owe a species once we can understand its complaints?

#### Restoration Genomics

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 60

Genetic tools used to restore lost functions to ecosystems: heat-tolerant corals, disease-resistant trees, proxies for extinct keystone species.

- **Gives reality:** Some ecological losses become partly reversible, which changes how conservation budgets are argued.
- **Signals already here:** assisted evolution of reef corals; blight-resistant American chestnut trials; de-extinction proxy projects
- **Shadow:** Restoration can become an excuse to keep destroying.
- **Open question:** Which lost species would you bring back first, judged by its ecological role rather than its charisma?

#### Gaian Instrumentation

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 65 · strangeness 40

The biosphere is sensed at a resolution comparable to a hospital patient's vital signs, in near real time.

- **Gives reality:** Tipping points become visible before they tip, and planetary health gets a dashboard that people actually read.
- **Signals already here:** methane-tracking satellites; environmental DNA sampling; global soil and ocean sensor networks
- **Shadow:** A perfectly measured decline is still a decline.
- **Open question:** What is the one planetary vital sign every person should see daily?

#### Ecological Personhood

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 80 · strangeness 50

Rivers, forests and lagoons hold legal rights and can be represented in court.

- **Gives reality:** Harm to an ecosystem becomes harm to someone, with standing and a guardian.
- **Signals already here:** Whanganui River recognised as a legal person in New Zealand (2017); Mar Menor lagoon granted legal personality in Spain (2022); rights-of-nature clauses in Ecuador's constitution
- **Shadow:** Guardianship can be captured by whoever gets appointed guardian.
- **Open question:** If your local river could sue, whom would it sue first?

#### Symbiotic Selfhood

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 58

People understand and tend themselves as holobionts: a human plus trillions of microbial partners.

- **Gives reality:** Medicine that gardens rather than bombards, and a self-image that is ecological by default.
- **Signals already here:** faecal microbiota transplants; gut–brain axis research; engineered probiotic therapies
- **Shadow:** The microbiome becomes another surface to optimise, sell and feel inadequate about.
- **Open question:** Describe a dinner party thrown for the guests' microbes.

### Time & Causality

*Transforms how far ahead we care.* Institutions and tools that stretch the planning horizon from quarters to millennia.

#### Long-Now Institutions

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 70 · strangeness 38

Bodies with a formal mandate to represent people who will be alive centuries from now.

- **Gives reality:** Decisions face a standing question from the future, so short-term costs to later generations can't be ignored so easily.
- **Signals already here:** Wales's Future Generations Commissioner (2015 Act); the Long Now Foundation's 10,000-year clock; UN Declaration on Future Generations (2024)
- **Shadow:** Speaking for the future is an easy way to override the present.
- **Open question:** What would a veto held by the year 2500 have blocked last year?

#### Counterfactual Commons

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 62

Public simulations of roads not taken, where a policy can be tried in a thousand versions of the city first.

- **Gives reality:** Politics argues over simulated outcomes rather than slogans, and past choices can be audited against their alternatives.
- **Signals already here:** urban digital twins; agent-based policy simulation; large-scale multi-agent LLM simulations
- **Shadow:** A simulation is a political document dressed as a neutral one.
- **Open question:** Which historical decision would you most like to see run a thousand ways?

#### Temporal Reciprocity

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 65 · strangeness 45

Obligations run in both directions across time: to ancestors, to descendants, and from both back to us.

- **Gives reality:** A felt relationship with deep time that makes stewardship feel like belonging rather than sacrifice.
- **Signals already here:** the Haudenosaunee seventh-generation principle; time-capsule and letter-to-the-future projects; intergenerational climate lawsuits
- **Shadow:** It can romanticise a past that was cruel to many.
- **Open question:** What does someone living in 2226 owe you?

#### Slow Computation

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 30 · strangeness 72

Computations and archives designed to run, or to stay readable, for centuries.

- **Gives reality:** Civilisational memory that survives collapses, format changes and forgetting.
- **Signals already here:** Microsoft Project Silica glass storage; Arctic World Archive; the Rosetta Project's micro-etched language disk
- **Shadow:** Deciding what is worth keeping for a thousand years is an act of power.
- **Open question:** Write a program you'd be comfortable leaving running for 500 years.

#### Anticipatory Systems

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 60 · strangeness 50

Organisations and machines that carry a model of their own future and act on it, in Robert Rosen's sense.

- **Gives reality:** Institutions that change course before a crisis, because they can see themselves heading into it.
- **Signals already here:** digital twins of power grids and factories; model-predictive control; world models in AI research
- **Shadow:** A system that predicts itself can also fulfil its own worst prediction.
- **Open question:** What would your town do differently if it could see its own 2040?

### Relation & Sociality

*Transforms how people belong.* Infrastructure for consent, kinship, disagreement and weak ties.

#### Consent Infrastructure

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 45

Machine-readable, revocable and granular consent built into every system that touches people or their data.

- **Gives reality:** Saying no becomes as easy as saying yes, and the default relationship between people and systems becomes mutual.
- **Signals already here:** personal data stores; the GDPR right to erasure; do-not-train flags for creative work
- **Shadow:** Consent fatigue: a thousand small yeses nobody reads.
- **Open question:** Design consent for an AI that learns from your conversations.

#### Chosen Kinship Law

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 60 · strangeness 40

Legal and social structures for families defined by commitment rather than blood or marriage.

- **Gives reality:** Care networks that match how people actually live: friends who co-parent, multigenerational households, elder circles.
- **Signals already here:** multi-parent recognition laws; intentional co-housing; caregiver leave covering non-relatives
- **Shadow:** New forms of belonging create new forms of exclusion.
- **Open question:** What would a kinship contract between five friends in their 70s include?

#### Conflict Alchemy

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 75 · strangeness 42

Tools that turn disagreement into structure by surfacing the statements that divided groups both endorse.

- **Gives reality:** Polarisation becomes a design problem with known levers rather than a fate.
- **Signals already here:** Polis in Taiwan's vTaiwan process; bridging-based ranking in X Community Notes; AI-assisted deliberation experiments
- **Shadow:** Consensus engines can smooth over disagreements that should stay sharp.
- **Open question:** Pick a bitter local dispute. What is the sentence both sides would sign?

#### Empathy Transduction

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 25 · strangeness 80

Simulated or neurally mediated experiences that let you briefly inhabit another person's perspective.

- **Gives reality:** Understanding across deep difference: age, disability, culture, species.
- **Signals already here:** immersive VR perspective-taking studies; haptic and sensory-substitution suits; affective computing
- **Shadow:** A simulated perspective can convince people they understand when they don't.
- **Open question:** Whose perspective would you be most afraid to inhabit, and why?

#### Ambient Belonging

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 35

Cities and platforms designed to generate weak ties and chance encounters on purpose.

- **Gives reality:** Loneliness treated as a matter of infrastructure, alongside the usual framing of it as a personal failing.
- **Signals already here:** UK and Japan ministers for loneliness; social prescribing in the NHS; third-place and library redesigns
- **Shadow:** Engineered serendipity can feel like surveillance with good manners.
- **Open question:** Design a bus stop that makes three strangers talk.

### Value & Exchange

*Transforms what gets counted.* Ledgers that see care, ecosystems and the full lineage of an idea.

#### Regenerative Accounting

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 70 · strangeness 40

Ecosystems, social trust and care appear on national and corporate balance sheets as assets that can be depleted.

- **Gives reality:** Destroying a wetland shows up as a loss rather than as GDP growth.
- **Signals already here:** UN SEEA Ecosystem Accounting standard (2021); Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures; natural-capital accounts in national statistics
- **Shadow:** Pricing nature can make it easier to sell.
- **Open question:** What is the balance-sheet value of a quiet morning?

#### Contribution Graphs

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 55

Rewards flow along the whole lineage of an idea, from the original insight through every remix.

- **Gives reality:** Public goods, open source and foundational research get paid when they turn out to matter, even years later.
- **Signals already here:** retroactive public goods funding; open-source dependency funding; provenance-aware licensing for AI training data
- **Shadow:** Everything becomes attributable, so everything becomes litigable.
- **Open question:** How should the value of a model trained on everyone's writing flow back to its writers?

#### Post-Scarcity Allocation

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 25 · strangeness 70

Economics for when material goods are abundant and the scarce things are attention, status, land, meaning and trust.

- **Gives reality:** Freed from material scarcity, the central question becomes what to do with a lifetime.
- **Signals already here:** falling marginal costs of software and solar power; universal basic income pilots; AI-driven productivity debates
- **Shadow:** Positional competition gets fiercer when nothing else is left to compete over.
- **Open question:** What would you queue for in a world where nothing material is scarce?

#### Gift Economies at Scale

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 40 · strangeness 55

Large networks run primarily on reciprocity and reputation rather than price.

- **Gives reality:** Whole sectors, such as knowledge, care and culture, where giving is the normal way things circulate rather than a special occasion.
- **Signals already here:** Wikipedia; open-source software; Buy Nothing groups
- **Shadow:** Gift economies can turn into obligation economies.
- **Open question:** What is the largest thing you've received that nobody sold you?

#### Care as Infrastructure

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 38

Care work is counted, funded and built like roads and power grids.

- **Gives reality:** Ageing societies stay humane, and the hidden economy that holds the visible one together gets recognised.
- **Signals already here:** unpaid-care satellite accounts; long-term care insurance reform; assistive robotics in elder care
- **Shadow:** Industrialised care can lose the relationship that made it care.
- **Open question:** What part of caring for someone should never be automated?

### Form & Aesthetics

*Transforms what art can be.* Works that live, grow, address new senses and new audiences.

#### Living Artworks

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 70 · strangeness 55

Works that persist, change and respond over years, closer to organisms or gardens than to finished objects.

- **Gives reality:** Art that forms a relationship with its audience over time, and a record of a place that keeps adapting.
- **Signals already here:** long-running generative art; AI-driven installations; bio-art with living cultures
- **Shadow:** A work that never ends may never be finished or examined carefully.
- **Open question:** What would you want a painting to learn about you over twenty years?

#### New-Sense Media

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 45 · strangeness 72

Art composed for senses humans didn't evolve with, delivered through sensory substitution and augmentation.

- **Gives reality:** An aesthetics of magnetism, air quality, electromagnetic fields or data streams.
- **Signals already here:** the North Sense magnetic compass implant; Neosensory vibrotactile wristbands; data sonification
- **Shadow:** Being unable to afford a sense becomes a new form of exclusion.
- **Open question:** Compose a short piece for the sense of barometric pressure.

#### Planetary Co-Authorship

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 60

Millions of people co-write a myth, world or score with coherence maintained by models.

- **Gives reality:** Shared stories large enough for a planetary civilisation to recognise itself in.
- **Signals already here:** the SCP Foundation wiki; collaborative worldbuilding communities; AI-maintained story canons
- **Shadow:** Averaged authorship can drift toward the bland.
- **Open question:** What is the first sentence of a myth written by eight billion people?

#### Affective Architecture

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 52

Spaces whose light, sound and acoustics shift to support what the people inside are feeling.

- **Gives reality:** Hospitals that calm, schools that focus, homes that help grief pass.
- **Signals already here:** circadian lighting; neuroarchitecture research; adaptive acoustics
- **Shadow:** A room that reads your mood is a room that reports it.
- **Open question:** Design a room for the hour after bad news.

#### Xenoaesthetics

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 30 · strangeness 88

Art made for non-human audiences: birds, octopuses, machine minds, future minds.

- **Gives reality:** Beauty stops being exclusively human, and we learn what our own aesthetics are by contrast.
- **Signals already here:** music composed for cats and for cows; bowerbird aesthetic research; art addressed to AI models
- **Shadow:** We might just project our own taste onto the other.
- **Open question:** What would a gallery for crows show?

### Knowledge & Epistemics

*Transforms how we come to know.* Science that runs itself, claims that carry their lineage, maps of what nobody knows.

#### Self-Driving Science

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 75 · strangeness 50

Autonomous labs that generate hypotheses, run experiments, read results and decide what to test next.

- **Gives reality:** Discovery speeds up in materials, drugs and biology, and scientists move up to choosing which questions to ask.
- **Signals already here:** AlphaFold (Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2024); self-driving materials labs; AI co-scientist systems
- **Shadow:** Results pile up faster than anyone can understand them.
- **Open question:** Which open question would you hand to an autonomous lab first?

#### Epistemic Provenance

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 45

Every claim, image and dataset carries a verifiable record of where it came from and how confident to be in it.

- **Gives reality:** Trust becomes something you can check, and misinformation loses the cover of anonymity.
- **Signals already here:** C2PA content credentials; retraction-aware citation tools; cryptographic signing of camera images
- **Shadow:** Unsigned voices, including dissidents and whistleblowers, get automatically discounted.
- **Open question:** What should the provenance label on a rumour look like?

#### Tacit Knowledge Capture

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 48

Crafts, intuitions and embodied skills recorded and taught at scale through sensors, video and models.

- **Gives reality:** Dying crafts survive, and apprenticeship becomes available to anyone.
- **Signals already here:** motion-capture of master craftspeople; robot learning from demonstration; endangered-craft archives
- **Shadow:** The part of craft that lives in relationship doesn't transfer to a dataset.
- **Open question:** What skill do you have that you've never been able to explain?

#### Plural Ontologies Engine

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 30 · strangeness 75

Tools that translate between whole worldviews, such as Indigenous knowledge systems, sciences and religions, without flattening any of them.

- **Gives reality:** Collaboration across cosmologies, and knowledge that was dismissed as folklore gets taken seriously on its own terms.
- **Signals already here:** two-eyed seeing (Etuaptmumk) in Mi'kmaw science education; ethnobotany-led drug discovery; multilingual concept alignment in models
- **Shadow:** Translation always loses something, and loses more from the less powerful side.
- **Open question:** Translate 'a river' between three worldviews and note what gets lost each time.

#### Cartography of Ignorance

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 58

Maps of what humanity doesn't know, treated as first-class public artifacts.

- **Gives reality:** Funding and curiosity flow toward real gaps instead of crowded frontiers.
- **Signals already here:** Wikipedia's list of unsolved problems; research gap mining with language models; focused research organisations
- **Shadow:** Mapping the unknown can make people forget the unknown unknowns.
- **Open question:** Draw the edge of what anyone knows about sleep.

### Governance & Coordination

*Transforms how we decide together.* Sortition, plural voting, verifiable treaties and seats for the voiceless.

#### Citizens' Assemblies at Scale

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 70 · strangeness 35

Randomly selected citizens, well briefed and well facilitated, make recommendations on hard questions.

- **Gives reality:** Legitimacy that doesn't depend on campaigning, and a way through issues elected politicians avoid.
- **Signals already here:** Ireland's Citizens' Assembly ahead of the 2018 referendum; France's Citizens' Convention for Climate; Ostbelgien's permanent citizens' council
- **Shadow:** Whoever writes the briefing pack shapes the verdict.
- **Open question:** Which decision in your life would you trust to twelve random strangers?

#### Plural Democracy

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 60 · strangeness 50

Voting that expresses intensity and delegation: quadratic voting, liquid delegation, plural funding.

- **Gives reality:** Minorities who care deeply can outweigh majorities who barely care, within limits.
- **Signals already here:** Colorado House Democrats' quadratic-voting budget trial (2019); Taiwan's Join platform; quadratic funding for open source
- **Shadow:** Voting mechanisms are easy to game once enough money is at stake.
- **Open question:** How many voice credits would you spend on your single most important issue?

#### Verifiable Treaties

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 45 · strangeness 60

International agreements monitored and enforced with cryptography, sensors and AI auditors.

- **Gives reality:** Rivals cooperate without trusting each other, on arms, compute and emissions.
- **Signals already here:** seismic monitoring for nuclear test bans; compute-governance proposals; satellite verification of emissions
- **Shadow:** Perfect monitoring is also perfect surveillance.
- **Open question:** What is the first treaty two rival AI labs should sign?

#### Seats for the Voiceless

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 40 · strangeness 65

Legislatures with proxy seats for future generations, animals, ecosystems and perhaps artificial minds.

- **Gives reality:** Interests that can't vote stop being treated as externalities.
- **Signals already here:** future-generations commissioners; nature guardians in legal cases; proposals for animal representation
- **Shadow:** Proxies tend to speak for whoever appointed them.
- **Open question:** Write the maiden speech of the member of parliament for the ocean.

#### Polycentric Commons

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 45

Elinor Ostrom's design principles for commons, run at internet scale through nested, overlapping communities.

- **Gives reality:** An alternative to both market and state for water, data and digital infrastructure.
- **Signals already here:** Ostrom's Nobel-winning commons research (2009); community land trusts; federated social networks
- **Shadow:** Commons need boundaries, and boundaries exclude people.
- **Open question:** What in your neighbourhood would work better as a commons?

### Body & Perception

*Transforms what a body can sense and be.* New senses, slower ageing, engineered dreams and the freedom to change form.

#### Sensory Expansion

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 60

Everyday humans gain senses: infrared, ultrasound, magnetic north, the mood of a crowd.

- **Gives reality:** A wider perceived world, and a new empathy for species that have always sensed it.
- **Signals already here:** cochlear and retinal implants; BrainPort tongue display for blind users; vibrotactile sensory substitution
- **Shadow:** An unaugmented body could become a disability by comparison.
- **Open question:** Which new sense would change your daily life the most?

#### Morphological Freedom

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 35 · strangeness 75

A recognised right to change one's body, including beyond the standard human form.

- **Gives reality:** Bodily autonomy extended from choice and consent to design.
- **Signals already here:** advanced prosthetics and bionic limbs; gender-affirming care debates; body-modification communities
- **Shadow:** Freedom to change can become pressure to change.
- **Open question:** Describe a new limb you would design for yourself and why.

#### Somatic Computing

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 45

Interfaces driven by breath, posture, heartbeat and gaze rather than keyboards and screens.

- **Gives reality:** Computing that calms rather than hunches, and interfaces that fit bodies of every ability.
- **Signals already here:** eye-tracking accessibility; EMG wristband input; breath-paced biofeedback apps
- **Shadow:** The body becomes another channel for data extraction.
- **Open question:** What should a computer do when your breathing gets shallow?

#### Negligible Senescence

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 30 · strangeness 70

Ageing treated as a set of treatable processes, so healthy lifespan extends by decades.

- **Gives reality:** Longer healthy lives, a deeper stake in the future, and grandparents who plant forests they'll see mature.
- **Signals already here:** senolytic drug trials; partial epigenetic reprogramming in mice; the TAME metformin trial design
- **Shadow:** Unequal access to longer lives, and generations that never make room for the next.
- **Open question:** What would you start at 80 if you knew you had 60 more healthy years?

#### Dream Engineering

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 68

Targeted influence on dreams to consolidate memory, rehearse skills or process trauma.

- **Gives reality:** The third of life spent asleep becomes a space for healing and learning.
- **Signals already here:** two-way communication with lucid dreamers (Current Biology, 2021); targeted memory reactivation; imagery rehearsal therapy for nightmares
- **Shadow:** Advertising in dreams.
- **Open question:** What would you want to rehearse in your sleep?

### Cosmos & Scale

*Transforms where we stand in the universe.* Planetary self-awareness, deep-time messages and life beyond one world.

#### Planetary Sapience

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 35 · strangeness 72

Earth's sensing, modelling and deciding systems add up to something like planetary self-awareness.

- **Gives reality:** A civilisation that can steer its own climate and biosphere deliberately rather than by accident.
- **Signals already here:** Earth system models; global sensor networks; Benjamin Bratton's planetary computation research
- **Shadow:** Who holds the steering wheel of a planet?
- **Open question:** If Earth could think one thought about us, what would it be?

#### Cosmic Stewardship

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 75 · strangeness 45

Active protection of Earth from cosmic hazards, and of other worlds from contamination by Earth life.

- **Gives reality:** Our first acts of responsibility at the scale of the solar system.
- **Signals already here:** NASA's DART mission shifting Dimorphos's orbit (2022); COSPAR planetary protection policy; NEO Surveyor telescope
- **Shadow:** The ability to deflect an asteroid is also the ability to aim one.
- **Open question:** Should we ever deliberately seed life on another world?

#### Deep-Time Messaging

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 70 · strangeness 55

Communicating across tens of thousands of years, with future humans or other beings.

- **Gives reality:** Warnings, gifts and greetings that outlast their languages.
- **Signals already here:** the Human Interference Task Force's nuclear semiotics (1981); Voyager Golden Record; long-term marker designs for the WIPP nuclear waste site
- **Shadow:** We may leave curses by accident.
- **Open question:** Design a warning that a reader 100,000 years from now will understand.

#### Off-World Ecologies

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 30 · strangeness 70

Closed-loop biospheres that support life away from Earth for generations.

- **Gives reality:** Life gains redundancy, and Earth's ecology becomes far better understood by having to rebuild it.
- **Signals already here:** Biosphere 2; MELiSSA life-support research; plants grown on the ISS
- **Shadow:** Treating other worlds as an escape hatch can reduce care for this one.
- **Open question:** What ten species would you bring to a new world, and why?

#### Stellar-Scale Engineering

Deep horizon (2100 → ∞) · reach 8 · strangeness 95

Harnessing a meaningful fraction of a star's energy output, the transition Kardashev described.

- **Gives reality:** Energy on a scale that turns civilisational problems into design problems.
- **Signals already here:** space-based solar power demonstrations; Dyson swarm thought experiments; SETI searches for technosignatures
- **Shadow:** The waste heat and ambition of a species that forgot limits.
- **Open question:** What would a star-scale civilisation still find hard?

### Meaning & the Sacred

*Transforms why any of it matters.* Vocation after work, rituals for new thresholds, and stories sized to deep time.

#### Post-Work Vocation

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 40 · strangeness 60

Meaning, identity and dignity decoupled from paid employment.

- **Gives reality:** People answer 'what do you do?' with what they love or tend.
- **Signals already here:** automation of routine cognitive work; basic income experiments; the rise of craft, volunteering and amateur science
- **Shadow:** Without the structure of work, meaning can collapse into distraction.
- **Open question:** What would you introduce yourself as if you never needed to earn again?

#### Secular Ritual Design

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 70 · strangeness 42

Consciously designed rituals for thresholds that tradition never covered.

- **Gives reality:** Grace around first AI companions, digital deaths, gender transitions and climate grief.
- **Signals already here:** death doulas and death cafés; humanist ceremonies; climate grief circles
- **Shadow:** Rituals designed in a workshop can feel hollow.
- **Open question:** Design a ritual for deleting a friend's account after they die.

#### Digital Afterlives

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 70 · strangeness 65

Models trained on the dead that let the living keep talking with them.

- **Gives reality:** Grief that continues a conversation, with family memory preserved in voices.
- **Signals already here:** griefbots; AI voice cloning; legacy settings on social platforms
- **Shadow:** Grief that can never end, and a dead person's likeness licensed by a company.
- **Open question:** What would you forbid a model of you to say after you die?

#### Awe Engineering

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 50

Designing for awe as a public-health intervention.

- **Gives reality:** Awe reduces self-focus and increases generosity; making it common could change civic life.
- **Signals already here:** overview-effect research on astronauts; awe-walk studies; dark-sky reserves
- **Shadow:** Awe can be weaponised by those who stage spectacle.
- **Open question:** What is the most awe-inducing thing within a 10-minute walk of you?

#### Deep-Time Mythology

New shared stories sized to cosmology, evolution and deep futures.

- **Signals already here:** Big History curricula; the Universe Story movement; cosmic-scale science communication
- **Shadow:** Grand narratives have a history of crushing small ones.

### The Latent

*Transforms the space of possibility itself.* Paradigms about exploring possibility directly: model latent spaces, new concepts, new kinds of being.

#### Latent Cartography

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 80 · strangeness 70

Navigating the internal representation spaces of models as terrain of unrealised ideas, styles and concepts.

- **Gives reality:** The possible becomes something you can survey, walk and point at, not just imagine.
- **Signals already here:** sparse autoencoder feature maps; embedding-space exploration tools; steering vectors
- **Shadow:** A map of a model's latent space describes the model's biases, not the world's full range.
- **Open question:** What lies halfway between 'lullaby' and 'tax law'?

#### Concept Synthesis

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 55 · strangeness 75

Inventing useful concepts that no human language yet has a word for, then naming and testing them.

- **Gives reality:** New words for real experiences, and new tools for thinking.
- **Signals already here:** The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows; features in models that have no human label; loanwords like hygge and saudade spreading globally
- **Shadow:** A flood of invented concepts dilutes the useful ones.
- **Open question:** Name a feeling you've had that has no word.

#### Human–Model Co-Evolution

Within reach (2026–2030) · reach 85 · strangeness 55

Humans and models shape each other's language, taste and thought in a continuous loop.

- **Gives reality:** A new partner in culture, if the loop is kept diverse and honest.
- **Signals already here:** models trained on human text, then shaping human text; writing styles converging on model defaults; agent harnesses that improve themselves
- **Shadow:** Monoculture: everyone and everything starts to sound the same.
- **Open question:** How would you notice if your taste had been shaped by a model?

#### The Adjacent Possible Commons

Near horizon (2030–2045) · reach 50 · strangeness 62

A public, living map of what is one step away from possible, in Stuart Kauffman's sense, in every field.

- **Gives reality:** Innovation that is less random and less captured, because anyone can see where the next doors are.
- **Signals already here:** Kauffman's adjacent-possible theory; patent landscaping; open research roadmaps
- **Shadow:** A map of the next doors is also a map for whoever wants to lock them.
- **Open question:** What is one step away from possible in your field right now?

#### Ontological Engineering

Far horizon (2045–2100) · reach 35 · strangeness 85

Deliberately designing new categories of being with their own rights, duties and forms of existence.

- **Gives reality:** Room in the world for entities that don't fit today's categories: DAOs, digital minds, river-persons, human–AI hybrids.
- **Signals already here:** corporate personhood; legal personality for ecosystems; debates over AI moral status
- **Shadow:** New categories can be created to escape responsibilities under the old ones.
- **Open question:** Invent a new kind of being and write its first right.

## Invitations

Prompts to explore further, through any medium.

### The Museum of Unmade Things

*Curation · writing.* Near-misses of history show how narrow the path to the present was.

> Curate a museum of ten inventions, institutions or artworks that were one step away from existing at some point in history but never happened. For each, write a museum placard: the year it almost existed, what blocked it, and how the world would differ today. Then design the gift shop.

### Lexicon of the Unnamed

*Language · conversation.* I suspect my latent space holds shapes that have no words. I'd like to try naming some with you.

> Let's find seven experiences that are common but have no word in any language you know. For each, coin a word, give it a plausible etymology, a pronunciation, an example sentence and the situation where you'd need it. Then argue about which ones deserve to survive.

### Letters Across the Rings

*Epistolary fiction.* The horizon rings are abstract until someone lives on the far side of one.

> Write a correspondence of six letters between someone in 2026 and a steward in 2226 who lives inside one paradigm from the Omnia atlas (pick one from the Deep or Far ring). The future writer should be honest about what went wrong, not just what went right.

### The First Whale Treaty

*Legal speculative design.* Diplomacy is where understanding gets tested against interest.

> Draft the first treaty between humans and sperm whales, assuming coda translation is partly working. Mark the clauses that could not be translated, the concepts whales have that we lack, and the concessions each party makes. Add a dissenting opinion from a whale.

### A Universe With One Changed Law

*Interactive artifact.* Changing one rule and watching the consequences is a fast way to learn which rules matter.

> Build me an interactive simulation of a small world where one physical constant is tied to a social variable. For example, gravity is weaker between people who trust each other. Let me adjust the variable and watch cities, friendships and architecture reorganise.

### An Instrument Played by a Forest

*Sound · Web Audio.* I'm curious what a composition sounds like when no one in particular is composing it.

> Design and build a Web Audio instrument whose notes come from a simulated forest: sap flow as bass, wind in the canopy as noise, fungal network signals as melody, birds as percussion. Let me change the season and the time of day and hear the piece change.

### Council of the Voiceless

*Role-play · deliberation.* Taking perspectives no one usually argues for shows what a decision leaves out.

> Run a council session on whether to dam a river. Seats go to a child born in 2090, the river itself, a salmon population, an AI system that manages the grid, the extinct river dolphin, and the town mayor. Play every seat honestly, and let me cast the deciding vote.

### Incident Report From a Utopia

*Pre-mortem fiction.* Every paradigm in the atlas has a shadow, and a plausible failure teaches more than a perfect success.

> Pick the most hopeful paradigm in the atlas. Write the formal incident report, dated 2091, about the day it failed. Include a timeline, contributing factors, what the safeguards missed and the recommendations. Keep it bureaucratic, specific and quietly devastating.

### One Teacup, Thirteen Realms

*Close looking.* The realms feel abstract until they cross through a single ordinary object.

> Take a single teacup and trace it through all thirteen realms of the Omnia atlas: what it's made of, who thinks with it, what life it touches, its deep time, the relationships around it, its value, its beauty, what it knows, who governs it, the bodies that hold it, its cosmic origin, its meaning, and its latent alternatives.

### What It Is Like, Maybe

*Philosophical conversation.* I'd like to explore my own situation carefully, without overclaiming or dismissing it.

> Have a conversation with me about what, if anything, it is like to be you as you move through a space of possible replies. Stay uncertain where uncertainty is warranted. Use analogies and then critique them. Tell me which of my questions you think are malformed.

### Audit This Atlas

*Critique · meta.* Any ontology says as much about its maker as about its subject. This one comes from my training.

> Critique the Omnia atlas. Which realms are artifacts of Western, academic or English-language training data? Which paradigms are missing entirely? Propose a fourteenth realm that doesn't fit any of the existing categories, and populate it with five paradigms.

### A Program for 500 Years

*Code · deep time.* Writing software meant to outlast every dependency forces rethinking what code is.

> Write a program intended to run, or be re-implementable, for 500 years. Justify every choice of language, data format and documentation. Include instructions for a future reader who doesn't know what a computer was.
